## 帖 1：r/Baking（内容钩子帖——首发主力）

**标题（二选一）：**
- A: `I cross-checked oven temperature charts from magnet makers, recipe sites and oven manuals. They don't agree.`
- B: `Your oven conversion chart might be lying to you — I compared 6 popular ones and found real disagreements`

**正文：**

```
After ruining a batch of cookies following a fridge-magnet chart (turns out it listed
130°C as 300°F — that's 266°F, a 34-degree error), I went down a rabbit hole and
started cross-checking the conversion charts you actually find in kitchens:

* a free magnet that came with a grocery order
* two big recipe sites' printable charts
* the conversion page in my oven's manual
* the UK consumer-testing site everyone links to

Things I found:

1. Gas Mark is NOT standardized across charts. Gas Mark 3 shows up as 160°C on some
   charts and 170°C on others. Both from sources you'd trust. (My dataset now lists
   both, with sources, instead of averaging them — averaging feels right but bakes wrong.)

2. Fan adjustments differ: most UK sources say fan ovens run ~20°C cooler, so
   200°C conventional = 180°C fan. But some charts apply it to Gas Mark conversions
   too, and some don't. That's a real difference for older recipes.

3. US→UK rounding is where cakes die. A recipe says 350°F. That's 176.7°C. Your knob
   has 170 and 180. Some charts say 175 (not on any UK knob I've used), some say 170,
   some say 180. Nobody explains which one they picked or why.

I ended up building a corrected table where every single cell carries its source,
plus a translator thing where you type what the recipe says + what kind of oven you
have, and it tells you what to actually set — including the rounding decision.

The table with sources is here: [LINK to fan-oven page / PDF]

Happy to answer "what about MY weird oven" questions in the comments — that's
genuinely the fun part.
```

**首评（发布后立即自评，放工具链接降低推广感）：**

```
Since a few people will ask: the interactive version (recipe says X, my oven is Y →
what do I set) lives here: [LINK]. Every cell cites its source — if you spot an
error, I'd genuinely rather know than be right.
```

**回评话术库：**
- 问"为什么不直接 Google"：`Google does the math right — 350°F IS 176.7°C. It just can't tell you whether your fan oven should be set to 160 or 170, because that's a convention question, not a math question. That's the part I tried to fix.`
- 问"你的数据哪来的"：`Every cell has a source line — mostly oven manufacturers' own manuals and the UK consumer test org. Where two credible sources disagree (Gas Mark 3), I list both instead of averaging. The full provenance is on the page.`
- 有人报错误数据：`Thanks — can you tell me which cell and which chart you're comparing? I'll re-verify against the source and update the table today.`（然后真去改，回来说已改）

## 帖 2：r/airfryer（工具帖——airfried.app 模式）

**标题：** `I made an oven-to-air-fryer translator that also handles fan ovens and Gas Mark`

**正文：**

```
Every frozen food box in my kitchen only has oven instructions. I kept re-Googling
the same two questions: how much to drop the temp, and how much to cut the time.

So I built a small tool: you type what the package or recipe says (°C, °F, Gas Mark,
fan or conventional — it figures out the rest), and it shows all four at once:
conventional, fan oven, Gas Mark, and air fryer, plus the adjusted time.

Rules it uses (all sourced on the page):
* fan/convection ≈ 20°C (25°F) lower than conventional
* air fryer ≈ same logic, time cut by ~20%
* rounding always lands on values that actually exist on UK/EU knobs

No signup, no ads, works on phone. [LINK]

If your air fryer runs hot/cold tell me the model and I'll note it — that's the
next thing I want to add.
```

**回评话术库：**
- "已经有 airfried.app 了"：`Yeah, and it's good! Mine mainly adds the fan/Gas Mark side and the rounding-to-real-knobs part for UK ovens. Happy to be the second tool if the first one solves you.`
